I.D.E.M.: Escaping Into Danger

Authorities have issued a renewed warning regarding unauthorized use of the I.D.E.M., or InterDimensional Escape Module, following the recovery of another deceased user near the Eastern Canal district.

The implant is commonly marketed through private security outlets as an emergency self-defense system: a last-resort means of escaping assault, robbery, or hostile pursuit by relocating the user out of immediate danger.

In practice, the outcome is far less controlled.

The I.D.E.M. teleports only its installed user, but the destination is not always safe. Investigators have linked recent deaths to users materializing inside mine shafts, beneath unstable structures, over deep ravines, inside flooded tunnels, and in locations already occupied by solid material or hostile creatures.

One witness described the system as “a panic button that rolls the dice with your life.”

Manufacturers continue to emphasize the implant’s success rate in removing users from active threats. Critics argue that these figures omit a more important question: what exactly the user escapes into.

Emergency clinics have reported a growing number of I.D.E.M.-related injuries, including fractures, drowning, exposure, blunt-force trauma, suffocation, and severe disorientation after relocation.

City safety officials recommend that residents avoid unlicensed I.D.E.M. installations and treat all emergency teleport hardware as hazardous medical equipment rather than guaranteed protection.

“It may get you away from the person trying to kill you,” said one rescue worker. “That does not mean it gets you somewhere safe.”
